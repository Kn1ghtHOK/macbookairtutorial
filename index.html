Every gesture.
One trackpad.

The Force Touch trackpad has no moving button. It clicks with haptic feedback and reads pressure and finger count, so one glass surface can do all of this.

Settings worth knowing

Open System Settings, then Trackpad. Each tab has a short video for every option.

Tap to click

Tap lightly instead of pressing down. It is off by default.

Trackpad › Point & Click

Natural scrolling

On by default, so the page follows your fingers like a phone. Switch it off for the reverse direction.

Trackpad › Scroll & Zoom

Secondary click

Choose a two-finger click, or click the bottom-right or bottom-left corner.

Trackpad › Point & Click

Click pressure and Quiet Click

Pick Light, Medium or Firm. Quiet Click silences the click sound.

Trackpad › Point & Click

Tracking speed

Slide to control how far the pointer travels for each swipe of your finger.

Trackpad › Point & Click

Three-finger drag

Turn on Use trackpad for dragging, then choose Three Finger Drag. This also controls tap-drag and drag lock.

Accessibility › Pointer Control › Trackpad Options

Three or four fingers

Choose which finger count triggers Mission Control, App Exposé and full-screen app swipes.

Trackpad › More Gestures

Zoom the screen

Turn on the scroll gesture with a modifier key to magnify the display.

Accessibility › Zoom

Gestures follow your macOS settings, and menu names shift a little between macOS versions. These apply to any Mac laptop with a Force Touch trackpad. Touch-screen gestures spotted in macOS 27.2 beta code, and touch input on an iPad used with Sidecar, are not trackpad features and are not covered. When in doubt, open System Settings › Trackpad.
